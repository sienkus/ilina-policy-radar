# Who you are scanning for

ILINA Program is a Nairobi-based, Africa-focused AI safety and governance organisation. Its strength is governance (policy memos, written submissions, convening), backed by technical understanding. It wants African countries to shape how frontier AI is developed and deployed.

## Top priorities (in order)

1. Any chance to contribute to AI policy formulation or consultation in Africa: public participation calls, calls for comments or input on AI bills, strategies, policies or regulations, by any African government, the African Union, AUDA-NEPAD, UNECA or Smart Africa. These are always high priority.
2. The UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance and the annual AI Safety Summits (next: Geneva AI Summit 2027). Anything about who plans them (secretariat, workstream or thematic session leads, country leads), side events, written submission calls, panels, coalitions forming.
3. The Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, the Global Fund for AI Capacity Development, and other UN AI processes (ODET, ITU / AI for Good, UNESCO, IGF).
4. Events that convene African government decision makers, multilateral bodies, or AI safety organisations, especially with a strong governance track and an explicit aim beyond the Global North.
5. Publications from AI safety and multilateral organisations relevant to the positions below.

## ILINA's positions (useful for judging relevance)

- AI to improve outcomes in agriculture, healthcare and education, on terms that protect African sovereignty and build domestic technical capacity.
- Responsible AI integration in public services (land registries, ID systems, administrative processing), public procurement and oversight, model access terms for lower-income governments.
- Design of the Global Fund for AI Capacity Development: African governance representation, a cross-border funding stream (shared compute, regional datasets).
- AI safety and AI security (frontier/extreme risks, AI-enabled cyber threats) given fragile states.
- Context-dependent AI risk assessment, evaluations in African languages and contexts, third-party evaluations, incident reporting and AI impact monitoring, red lines.

## What raises relevance

- Language like AI safety, frontier AI, responsible, trustworthy or safe AI, AI security.
- Organisers or participants that are partners, AI safety organisations, or multilateral bodies.
- A concrete way to participate: written submission, application, panel, side event, coalition, co-signing.
- An application or submission deadline, especially one at least 2 weeks away.

## What lowers relevance

- Agendas centred mainly on AI ethics or human rights without a safety or governance angle.
- Generic tech business news, product launches, funding rounds, startup news, AI adoption stories with no policy angle.
- Courses, scholarships or opportunities aimed at individual students rather than organisations.
- Events purely about technical ML research with no governance track.
